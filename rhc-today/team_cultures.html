🦃 A November Story
A Turkey Walks Into the Clinic

It was the Monday before Thanksgiving, and the clinic was humming along. The coffee was hot, the schedule was full, and nobody suspected a thing. Then, at 8:02 AM, the automatic doors slid open and in strutted a very large, very confident wild turkey.

The front desk looked up. The turkey looked back. "Do you have an appointment?" someone asked, mostly out of habit. The turkey did not. The turkey did, however, have a plan, and the plan appeared to be a full lap of the building.

🦃 🍂 🦃

Down the hallway he went, feathers puffed and tail fanned, gobbling at the hand sanitizer dispenser as if it had said something rude. A medical assistant rounded the corner with a vitals cart and froze. The turkey froze. For one long moment, nobody moved. Then the blood pressure cuff on the cart gave a loud hiss, and the turkey bolted straight into Exam Room Two.

Within minutes, half the clinic had gathered in the hallway. A nurse offered a graham cracker. A provider suggested a calm and reassuring voice. Someone from the back office quietly started a new patient intake form. Name: Gerald. Reason for visit: "Hiding. Please do not tell the oven." Temperature: 105 degrees, which, it turns out, is perfectly normal for a turkey.

🍁 🥧 🍁

In the end, it took an open side door, a trail of crackers, and a team that works together better than anyone expected. Gerald was gently guided back outside. He paused at the threshold, gave the whole clinic one last dignified gobble, and strutted off toward the mountains.

Gerald never came back for a follow-up, but he left us with a lesson. Everyone pitched in, nobody panicked, and the team handled the unexpected with a sense of humor. Not a bad way to start Thanksgiving week.

This story is entirely fictional. No turkeys were harmed, and none have requested a refill.

🍂 November: Did You Know?

November comes from the Latin word "novem," meaning nine. It was the ninth month of the ancient Roman calendar, back when the year began in March.

🦃 Wild turkeys can run up to about 25 miles per hour and can fly short distances at around 55 miles per hour.
🌡️ A turkey's normal body temperature is around 105 degrees Fahrenheit, which is a few degrees warmer than ours.
🥧 Thanksgiving became an official national holiday in 1863, when President Abraham Lincoln proclaimed a national day of thanks.
🏔️ By November, the high country around Pagosa Springs is often getting its first lasting snow, so winter driving season is just around the corner.
