Internal Memo · Patient Experience

Communication MattersEvery interaction counts

To:  Patient Service Representatives (PSRs)

As Patient Service Representatives, you are often the first and last person a patient interacts with during their visit. The way we communicate has a lasting impact on how patients feel about their care, regardless of the clinical outcome.

Patients may not always remember exactly what was said, but they will remember how they were treated.

01 Common Communication Concerns

Some of the most frequent patient complaints across healthcare organizations are not related to medical care. Instead, they often involve perceptions such as:

  • Feeling rushed
  • Feeling ignored
  • Feeling like they are an inconvenience
  • Receiving short or abrupt responses
  • Not being greeted warmly
  • Feeling that staff were distracted or uninterested

While these concerns may not reflect our intentions, they can significantly affect a patient's experience.

02 How Patients May View Our Interactions

What may seem routine to us may feel very different to a patient.

Staff PerspectivePatient Perspective
"I'm busy.""They don't have time for me."
"I'm being efficient.""They are rushing me."
"I've answered this question many times today.""They seem annoyed."
"I'm focused on my work.""They are not listening."
Our goal is to ensure patients feel welcomed, respected, and supported during every interaction.

03 Communication Best Practices

Greet Every Patient

  • Make eye contact when possible
  • Smile
  • Acknowledge the patient promptly
  • Use a friendly greeting

Listen First

  • Allow patients to finish speaking
  • Avoid interrupting
  • Clarify concerns when needed

Use Positive Language

Instead of"That's not my job." → Try"Let me find the right person to help you."
Instead of"You'll have to wait." → Try"We'll get you checked in as quickly as possible."

Be Mindful of Tone

Patients hear our tone before they process our words. A calm, friendly tone can de-escalate concerns and create trust.

Remember the Human Side

Patients may be:

Sick
In pain

A small act of kindness can make a significant difference.
