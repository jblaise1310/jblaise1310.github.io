Crisis Resources & Clinic Protocols

Primary Care Clinic
When a patient is in crisis, speed and clarity matter. Every staff member should know our key crisis resources and what steps to take when a patient discloses suicidal thoughts, before a crisis moment happens.
🎯
Our Clinic Goal Every patient, every visit. The C-SSRS is our standardized screening tool. It gives every patient an equal opportunity to be heard, and gives our team a consistent language for assessing and responding to risk.

📋 About the C-SSRS: Columbia Suicide Severity Rating Scale

The C-SSRS is our clinic's suicide risk screening tool. It uses a series of straightforward questions to assess whether a patient is experiencing suicidal ideation or has a history of suicidal behavior. It is validated across diverse populations and languages.

Based on the patient's responses, the C-SSRS guides the next clinical step:

Low / No Ideation Continue routine care. Document and monitor.
Moderate Ideation Provider review required. Behavioral health referral. Safety planning.
High / Active Ideation with Plan Immediate provider notification. Do not leave patient alone. Crisis protocol activates.

What To Do When a Patient Discloses

If a patient discloses suicidal thoughts or you suspect they may be in distress, follow these steps:

  1. 1
    Stay calm and listen Thank the patient for sharing. Do not minimize or change the subject. A calm, caring presence reduces distress immediately.
  2. 2
    Ask directly "Are you thinking about suicide?" Asking directly does not increase risk. It opens the door and shows the patient that you can handle the conversation.
  3. 3
    Alert a provider immediately Do not leave the patient alone. Notify the provider or charge nurse right away so a clinical assessment can happen before the patient leaves.
  4. 4
    Document what you heard Use the patient's own words when possible. Note the time, what was said, and who was notified. This is essential for continuity of care.
  5. 5
    Ensure a warm handoff or follow-up plan The patient should leave with a clear next step, a referral, a safety plan, or a follow-up call. They should not exit without a plan in place.

🔴 Immediate Danger

If a patient indicates they have a plan, access to means, or intend to act imminently, call 911 or activate the emergency protocol immediately. Do not allow the patient to leave unsupported.

Remember This

You do not have to be a clinician to make a difference in a crisis moment. Your job is to stay present, get help quickly, and make sure the patient is never left alone. The response starts with you.
