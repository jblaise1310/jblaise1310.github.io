Recognizing Risk Factors & Warning Signs

Primary Care Clinic
Suicide is preventable. As primary care staff, you are often the first and sometimes the only clinical contact for patients in crisis. Knowing what to look for can make a life-saving difference.
🎯
Our Clinic Goal Screen every patient, every visit using the Columbia Suicide Severity Rating Scale (C-SSRS). Consistent screening is how we identify patients who need support before a crisis occurs.

Warning Signs to Watch For

These may appear in conversation, behavior, or patient records. Trust your observations and act on them.

💬
Talking about wanting to die "I wish I wasn't here" or "Everyone would be better off without me"
😔
Hopelessness or feeling trapped Expressed belief that things won't get better; no way out
⚡
Increased agitation or reckless behavior Sudden mood changes, risk-taking, giving away belongings
🚫
Withdrawal from people and activities Social isolation, canceling appointments, stopping medications
🔴
Direct mention of a plan or means Access to firearms, medications, or a specific stated plan. Act immediately.
😴
Dramatic changes in sleep or eating Extreme insomnia or sleeping all day; significant weight changes
Key Risk Factors

Clinical / Mental Health

  • Depression or bipolar disorder
  • Anxiety disorders
  • Substance use disorder
  • Chronic pain or illness
  • Traumatic brain injury
  • Prior suicide attempt (strongest predictor)
  • Recent psychiatric hospitalization

Situational / Social

  • Recent loss or grief
  • Housing instability
  • Financial crisis
  • Social isolation
  • Relationship breakdown
  • Legal or occupational problems
  • Access to lethal means (especially firearms)
  • Discrimination or marginalization

Risk factors inform, they do not predict.

No checklist can definitively identify who will attempt suicide. Many patients have multiple risk factors and never reach crisis; some with few risk factors do. Use risk factors to guide conversations, screenings, and referrals rather than to make assumptions about patients.

Your Action as Staff

If you observe a warning sign or a patient discloses distress, you do not have to handle it alone. Tell a provider, document what you observed, and ensure the patient does not leave without a referral or safety check. Every role in this clinic plays a part.
