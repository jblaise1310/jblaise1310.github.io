When Should a Referral Happen?
😔
Any mention of hopelessness or suicidal thoughts Even vague statements warrant a clinical check-in and referral consideration
😰
Elevated C-SSRS or depression screening scores PHQ-9 scores of 10 or above are a threshold for behavioral health follow-up
💊
Substance use concerns Any disclosure of risky alcohol or drug use warrants at minimum a brief intervention
😤
Chronic stress, grief, or life disruption Job loss, bereavement, domestic violence, or caregiver strain all elevate risk
🏥
New chronic diagnosis or worsening condition Patients adjusting to major illness often need emotional support, not just medical care
🔄
Repeated visits with unexplained symptoms Frequent high-utilizers with no clear medical cause may be experiencing mental health distress
What Is a Warm Handoff?

A warm handoff means personally connecting the patient to the next person in their care, not just handing them a number or a paper referral.

  • Introduce the behavioral health team member by name. Even a brief introduction dramatically increases follow-through and removes the feeling that patients are being handed off or dismissed.

  • Frame the referral positively. "We want to make sure you have as much support as possible" is more welcoming than "we're referring you for mental health."

  • Give them something tangible before they leave. A phone number, a scheduled follow-up appointment, or a printed resource. Remove as many steps as possible between the patient and care.

  • Document the referral and the patient's response. Did they agree? Express hesitation? This informs the follow-up plan and ensures continuity.

Addressing Common Patient Hesitations

What patients often say

"I don't need therapy." / "I can handle it myself." / "I can't afford it." / "I don't have time." / "That's not really my thing."

What staff can say

"It's just a conversation; you can see what you think." / "We have options that may fit your schedule and budget." / "A lot of our patients find it really helpful even after just one visit."

The referral gap is real and closeable.

Research consistently shows the biggest drop-off in behavioral health access happens between the referral and the first appointment. Warm handoffs, reduced wait times, and proactive follow-up calls have all been shown to close this gap meaningfully. These are not extra steps; they are the care.

Your Action as Staff

Know what behavioral health services are available at our clinic and in the community. When you offer a referral, make it easy, not just available. And if a patient hesitates, note it and let the care team know so follow-up can happen.
