Our Zero Suicide Commitment

Reflecting on the year and looking ahead
Primary Care Clinic
You have completed the first year of our Zero Suicide staff education series. Every issue you read, every conversation it sparked, and every patient you looked at a little more closely — that is how Zero Suicide becomes real in our clinic.
🎯
Our Clinic Goal Every patient, every visit. The C-SSRS is our standard. Consistent screening across all staff and all visits is how we ensure every patient has an equal chance to be seen, heard, and supported.

Why Underserved Populations Remain a Focus

90%

of counties in the U.S. have a shortage of mental health providers, disproportionately affecting rural and low-income communities

2.5x

American Indian and Alaska Native youth have a suicide rate 2.5 times higher than the national average for youth

1 in 3

LGBTQ+ individuals who sought mental health care report being treated poorly or turned away because of their identity

58%

of people in the U.S. who die by suicide are non-Hispanic white, but suicide rates are rising fastest among Black youth and American Indian populations

Zero Suicide Is a Commitment, Not a Program

Zero Suicide is a philosophy: that suicide deaths among people in our care are preventable, and that every member of the care team plays a role. It is built on five pillars.

🎓
Train the Workforce
🔍
Identify Patients at Risk
🤝
Engage and Involve
🛡️
Treat Suicidal Distress
🔗
Transition Care Safely
Where We Are Headed
  • 📋

    Strengthening our workflows: We are reviewing how behavioral health referrals happen in our clinic to identify gaps and create more consistent pathways for patients in distress.

  • 🎯

    Maintaining our screening standard: Our goal remains every patient, every visit using the C-SSRS. We are working to ensure this becomes a consistent, documented part of every care encounter.

  • 🌐

    Improving access for underserved patients: We are exploring ways to reduce barriers to behavioral health care for the patients in our community who face the most obstacles.

  • 📣

    Inviting your input: Your observations matter. If you notice a gap, a barrier, or a patient who slipped through, say something. You are part of our quality improvement process.
