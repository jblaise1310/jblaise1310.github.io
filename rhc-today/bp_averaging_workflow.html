Workflow Reference — Standard Approach (RHC)

Home Blood Pressure Averaging

Clinical Staff Reference Guide
📋 RN Function
Effective date: 04/16/26 Prepared by: Jo Blaise

Purpose

This workflow standardizes how home blood pressure readings are collected, reviewed, averaged, and documented. The home BP averaging process is an RN function. Other staff should be aware of this workflow to support patients and understand the documentation process. Consistent home BP documentation supports accurate hypertension management, reduces reliance on single in-office readings, and improves our performance on the CMS 165 Controlling High Blood Pressure eCQM measure.

Why this approach

  • Reflects the patient's current blood pressure control
  • Reduces variability from older or inconsistent readings
  • Aligns with AHA, Stergiou, and NICE/RACGP guidance
  • Improves clinical decision-making and medication titration

How to calculate the average

  1. Select the data Use the most recent 2–7 days of readings (example range — follow provider instructions). Minimum of 1 reading per day. (Adjust per provider clinical judgment)
  2. Exclude first day Remove all readings from Day 1 of the selected window.
  3. Calculate the average Average all systolic values, then average all diastolic values.
  4. Document *

    Document in Cerner using the Blood Pressure AdHoc form. Record avg. BP reading, site, date of last BP reading, and comment:

    Home BP average: ___ / ___ mmHg based on X days of readings (Day 1 excluded). Date range: MM/DD/YY–MM/DD/YY.

    Charting reminders: Use the last date of the patient's BP entries as the date on the form. Time can be entered as 0000.

* Coming soon — Copilot Home BP Averaging Tool: Once validated, staff will copy and paste the patient's home BP log directly into the Copilot agent. The agent will perform the averaging and generate the documentation comment automatically. Staff will then chart the result on the Blood Pressure AdHoc form in Cerner using the last date of BP entries and time 0000.

Example

Patient provides 7 days of readings (April 1–7)

  • Exclude: April 1
  • Use: April 2–7 (6 days)

Document on the Blood Pressure AdHoc form in Cerner:

Home BP average: 132/84 mmHg based on 6 days of readings (Day 1 excluded). Date range: 04/02/26–04/07/26.
Minimum acceptable data: At least 3 days of readings.

When to start a new monitoring period

Reset and apply the same method when:

  • Medication is started or adjusted
  • BP control is being reassessed
  • Patients are newly instructed to monitor
📌 Best practice — new patients: Patients who are new to home monitoring should bring their device to their next appointment so the care team can confirm they are performing readings correctly before relying on the data.

Common pitfalls (avoid)

Using a single reading
Averaging only one day
Including Day 1 readings
Using outdated readings instead of recent data
Not documenting date range and method

Clinical interpretation reminder

Threshold Interpretation
Home BP ≥ 140/90 mmHg Consistent with hypertension (clinic threshold)
