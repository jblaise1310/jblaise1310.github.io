Director analysis — summary for leadership review
✓ Doing well
Clinical volume is strong. Primary care Q4 avg 12.1 visits/day; RSM (13.3), Nobles (13.8), and Zappone (13.8) all performing above benchmark. McIntyre's panel grew +32.7% in 4 months — fastest in the group.
Steed (Orthopedics) ramping effectively. Surgical cases grew from 16 YTD in 2024 to 92 YTD in 2025 — a 475% increase in first full year. Provider is engaged, positive, and wants more volume.
Abbas (Behavioral Health) building steadily. BH visits grew from 39 in July to 76 in October — 95% growth in 4 months. Zero after-hours EHR time. High satisfaction with support and team culture.
AI adoption gaining traction. 5 providers actively using documentation tools (Freed, Dragon, Cerner AI). Multiple others expressed interest. Measurable after-hours reduction reported by Aragon and Liverett.
Support staff praised consistently across the practice. Kinsey, Katy, Peyton, David, Charlene, Priscilla, and Tori received specific positive mentions across multiple providers — a retention and morale asset.
Brown and Holcomb maintain zero after-hours EHR time. Brown's visit volume grew 75% Jul→Oct. Holcomb happy, praises team, wants more patient volume.
⚠ Monitor closely
McIntyre after-hours charting is climbing. Went from 6.2% in July to 13.8% in October — a 121% increase in one quarter. Panel growth (+32.7%) is outpacing documentation support. Without intervention this trajectory leads to burnout.
Nobles after-hours consistently high. Averaging 18.8% after-hours EHR time in Q4, spiking to 22.5% in September. Over-documentation habit driven by prior malpractice case. AI pilot could be high-impact here.
Mashburn and Zappone message center % elevated. Zappone averaging 25.2% and Mashburn 24.7% of total EHR time in message center — well above peers (RSM 18.3%, McIntyre 8.7%). RN triage quality is a likely contributor.
Webb (Orthopedics) surgical volume declining. Down from 153 cases YTD in 2024 to 109 in 2025 (−29%). October dropped to just 1 case. Provider did not flag this in interview — needs clarification: scheduling, referral patterns, or capacity choice?
Tinguely (General Surgery) shared RN model is unsustainable. Provider explicitly stated RN divided among 3 providers is not feasible. Room stocking failures. Encounter volume dropped to 9 in October — low.
Liverett visit ramp may mask fragility. Visits/day grew 9.3→13.0 Jul→Oct (fastest growth in group) but provider flagged turnover stress, no retention incentives, and after-hours compensation concerns.
19 open action items from Q3/Q4. Several (refill workflow, PDMP access, TCM billing, pediatric screening training, BH visit limits) remain unstarted. Providers will notice if items raised in 1:1s are not visibly progressed before the next cycle.
✗ Genuine concern — action required
Leadership/c-suite disconnect is a retention risk. Zappone, Nobles, Liverett, Tinguely, and Mashburn independently cited feeling disconnected from, undervalued by, or unheard by c-suite. This pattern across 5 providers — spanning Primary Care, Specialty, and multiple tenures — is systemic, not individual.
Registration/check-in dysfunction is clinic-wide and unresolved. 8 of 14 providers named it as a primary bottleneck. No provider reported it as resolved. The PI project started Q3 has not yet produced perceptible improvement at the provider level.
Tinguely (General Surgery) expresses no confidence in feedback channels. "No one has power to change anything. Who is looking out for the doctors?" Combined with room stocking failures and an October encounter count of 9 — this provider's engagement is at risk.
Compensation equity for after-hours work is unresolved. Nobles and Liverett specifically cited hourly pay structure and lack of compensation for work outside scheduled hours. Nobles framed it as a contract fairness issue. This is a retention vulnerability.
Zappone's disengagement signal should not be dismissed. Long-tenured, multi-role provider. "It feels unsafe/threatening to speak up." Still engaged with patient care but organizational loyalty is eroding. Both a turnover risk and a culture signal for peers who observe it.
