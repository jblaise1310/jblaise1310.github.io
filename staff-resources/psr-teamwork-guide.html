Building Positive Working Relationships

A short guide to how we treat one another, so that the people we serve always meet a team that is steady, kind, and working together.

Our Goal

A workplace where people feel respected, supported, and valued.

The work we do is challenging. We serve patients who are often frustrated, worried, sick, or confused. Because of that, it matters that we create a work environment where team members feel respected, supported, and valued.

A positive workplace does not require everyone to be best friends. It does require professionalism, courtesy, and mutual respect.

Eight Practices

The habits that build a team worth being part of

1

Speak About Coworkers as if They Were Standing Next to You

A simple rule: if you would not say it directly to the person, do not say it to someone else.

Before discussing a coworker, ask yourself:

  • Is this helpful?
  • Is this necessary?
  • Have I spoken directly to the person first?
  • Would I be comfortable if they overheard this conversation?

Whenever possible, leave coworkers' names out of conversations unless there is something positive, supportive, or work-related that needs to be discussed.

Redirecting in the Moment

When the Conversation Turns

You do not have to take part in a conversation that turns negative, and you do not have to wait for it to run its course. If someone begins to speak poorly about another person, you can change its direction in a single sentence. A gentle redirect is not rude. It is one of the kindest, most professional things you can do.

Can we change the subject?
I would rather not talk about someone like that.
I try not to discuss people who are not here to speak for themselves.
Have you been able to talk with them directly?
Let's focus on something we can actually fix.
I would like to keep things positive today.

Said warmly, these phrases end the conversation without ending the friendship. Most people will follow your lead.

2

Address Concerns Directly and Professionally

Misunderstandings happen. If a coworker does something that frustrates you:

  • Assume positive intent first.
  • Seek clarification before making assumptions.
  • Have a respectful conversation if appropriate.
  • If the issue cannot be resolved, bring it to leadership.
Lean Into
  • Going to the person first
  • Clarifying questions
  • Solving it once
Step Away From
  • Gossip
  • Side conversations
  • Recruiting others to take sides
  • Repeatedly rehashing the issue
3

Focus on Behaviors, Not Personalities

Labels are rarely productive. Statements like "she is difficult," "he is lazy," or "they always do this" close a conversation before it starts. Specific behaviors and facts open it back up.

Instead of a label
  • "She is difficult."
  • "He is lazy."
  • "They always do this."
Try a fact
  • "I need clarification on this process."
  • "I was unsure how this task should be completed."
  • "There seems to be a communication breakdown."

Professional conversations focus on solutions rather than labels.

4

Give Grace

Everyone has difficult days. On any given shift, a coworker may be managing:

  • Personal stress
  • Family responsibilities
  • Health concerns
  • Heavy workloads
  • Challenging patient interactions

A short response or a frustrated moment does not automatically mean someone intended harm. Give people the same grace you would want extended to you.

5

Keep Patient Care at the Center

When disagreements arise, there is one question that settles most of them:

"What is best for the patient?"

Patient care should always outweigh territorial behavior, personal preferences, hurt feelings, and department politics. We are one team serving the same patients.

6

Help When You Can

Workloads fluctuate throughout the day. If your work is caught up and another team member is struggling:

  • Offer assistance.
  • Ask if help is needed.
  • Share knowledge.
  • Support one another.

Success is measured by how well the team performs, not by how much work an individual protects.

7

Maintain Professional Communication

The message matters, but the tone matters too.

This is professional
  • Respectful tone
  • Active listening
  • Constructive feedback
  • Calm problem-solving
This is not
  • Sarcasm
  • Dismissive comments
  • Public criticism
  • Talking about coworkers behind their backs
8

Be Part of the Solution

Every employee contributes to workplace culture. A few honest questions keep us pointed the right way:

Am I improving the environment today? Am I helping solve problems? Am I bringing concerns to the right people? Am I treating coworkers with respect?

Culture is built one interaction at a time.

Final Thought

A strong team does not avoid conflict. It addresses conflict respectfully, professionally, and directly.

We will not always agree. We will not always work the same way. We may occasionally frustrate one another.

What matters is that we remain respectful, communicate professionally, support one another, and keep our focus where it belongs: providing exceptional care to our patients.
