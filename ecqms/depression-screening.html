eCQM: Depression Screening

Why we screen every patient, how to chart it, and how a single question can open the door to care.
Section 1

Why It Matters

Depression is common, serious, and treatable, and the visit in front of you is often the only door in.

The quiet diagnosis

Most people living with depression never raise it at a medical visit. The symptoms (low mood, fatigue, loss of interest, hopelessness) are easy to attribute to stress, age, or "just a rough patch." That is exactly why we don't wait for patients to bring it up. We ask everyone, every time. A single screening question can be the moment a person first feels seen.

Why the screen matters most here. Colorado has long ranked among the hardest states in which to access mental health care. For many patients, a routine visit is the only point of contact with the health system all year, so a screen done at the point of care is often the first, and sometimes the only, chance to identify someone and connect them to help.

Who we screen

  • Every patient age 12 and older.
  • Every patient. Not just those who seem low, and not just behavioral-health visits.
  • Every visit. The screen is part of the standard visit, the same way vitals are.

Your part is the first step in the chain. An accurate screen and a clean handoff to the provider are what turn a quiet struggle into a plan of care.

Dolores didn't come in for her mood, and she may never raise it on her own. The depression screen is built into her visit precisely so moments like this aren't missed.

Your turn: Dolores came in for her blood pressure, not her mood. In your own words, why do we still screen her, and what might a routine screen catch that the visit otherwise wouldn't?

A strong answer would include

Most people with depression never bring it up at a medical visit, and grief, fatigue, and loss of interest can be early signs. Screening every patient at every visit means we catch it in people who would otherwise go unidentified, like Dolores, whose recent loss and fatigue are worth a closer look. The screen is the door in; without it, her visit would close on her blood pressure alone.

Section 2

The Measure

What CMS2v14 actually asks of us, and why both halves matter.

What an eCQM is

An eCQM (electronic Clinical Quality Measure) is a standardized way of showing that the right care happened and was documented. CMS2v14 is the depression screening measure. We earn credit for it only when both halves are done and charted correctly.

Measure Description · eCQM CMS2v14

Percentage of patients aged 12 years and older screened for depression on the date of the encounter using an age-appropriate standardized screening tool, AND, if positive, a follow-up plan documented on the date of the encounter or within 2 calendar days.

The two halves

  • Half one, the screen. Every eligible patient is screened on the date of the encounter using the right standardized tool.
  • Half two, the follow-up plan. If the screen is positive, a follow-up plan is documented the same day or within 2 calendar days. The follow-up plan is a provider action: they may refer to Behavioral Health, change a patient's medications, or take other clinical actions.
Both halves, or no credit. A completed screen alone is not enough. If a screen is positive and the follow-up plan isn't documented in the window, the measure is missed, and more importantly, the patient may not get the next step they need.

The eligible population

Age: 12 and older
Who: every patient
When: every visit
Window if positive: same day or within 2 calendar days
Apply ItDolores Hartley, 67
Dolores screens positive (PHQ-9 = 12). It's 4:55 PM on a Friday, and the provider has already left for the day.

A positive screen starts a clock. The follow-up plan has to be documented on the date of the encounter or within 2 calendar days.

Your turn: The provider is gone and it's Friday afternoon. Does the measure give you until Monday? What do you need to make sure happens, and by when?

A strong answer would include

The window is 2 calendar days. It runs continuously and includes the weekend, so this isn't "the next business day." The follow-up plan and provider notification need to be in the chart within that window, not on Monday. You'd follow your escalation path to reach the covering or on-call provider so the plan is documented in time, rather than letting it slip past the weekend.

Section 3

Screening the Patient

Pick the right tool, run it completely, and let the system do the scoring.

Step 1 · Choose the right intake form

The tool you open depends on the patient's age. Start here, every time:

Child / Adolescent
PHQ-2 / PHQ-A · patients 12 to 17 years of age
Adult
PHQ-2 / PHQ-9 · patients 18 and older, including any pregnant or postpartum patient, regardless of age

Step 2 · Complete the screening

  • Complete the PHQ in its entirety. The patient may self-complete it, or you may administer it verbally.
  • Enter responses exactly as the patient gives them. The system calculates the score.
  • Confirm you have a Total Depression Screening Score before moving on.

Understanding How the PHQ is scored

Each item is rated on the same 0–3 scale:

0  Not at all
1  Several days
2  More than half the days
3  Nearly every day
  • PHQ-2: two questions (little interest or pleasure; feeling down, depressed, or hopeless). Range 0–6.
  • PHQ-9 / PHQ-A: the two PHQ-2 items plus seven more (sleep, energy, appetite, concentration, movement, self-worth, and thoughts of self-harm). Range 0–27.
Our standard: never stop at the PHQ-2. All patients continue to the full PHQ-9 or PHQ-A regardless of the PHQ-2 score. The PHQ-2 is not a gate. It is the first two questions of the same screen.

Section 4

Reading the Score

What each score band means, and the one rule that overrides the total.

Score → action

Act on the patient's total PHQ-9 / PHQ-A score, not the PHQ-2 alone.

0–4No depression
No further action unless the provider requests it.
5–9Mild depression
Notify the provider. Document the notification in the Communication Book. Provide education if appropriate. Follow provider orders.
10+Moderate to severe
Notify the provider immediately. Document in the Communication Book. Provide education if appropriate. Follow provider orders: may include integrated behavioral health, a BH referral, transfer to the emergency department, or other.

The rule that overrides the total

Item 9: thoughts of self-harm. If the patient answers anything other than "Not at all" to item 9, notify the provider immediately, regardless of the total score. A low total never makes this safe to wait on.

Two questions that aren't scored

  • Functional impact question. Not scored, but it gives the provider important context on how symptoms affect work, home, and relationships.
  • Postpartum question. Asked of all patients who have delivered within the past 12 months. It is for informational purposes only. No additional scoring tool is required.
Apply ItDolores Hartley, 67
Dolores's total PHQ-9 comes to 4, which is low. But on item 9, she answered "Several days" to having thoughts of self-harm.

The total score says "low." Item 9 says otherwise. One rule overrides the total.

Your turn: Her total is only 4. Do you treat this as a negative screen? What do you do, and why?

A strong answer would include

No, a low total does not make this safe. Any answer other than "Not at all" on item 9 (thoughts of self-harm) requires notifying the provider immediately, regardless of the total score. You would notify the provider right away and document it, rather than letting the low total close the screen.

Section 5

Documenting It

The assessment is always documented; the provider notification, only when the score calls for it.

Step 1 · Document the Depression Screening in your intake form

Document the Depression Screening within your intake form. This is done for every patient you screen, regardless of the score.

Step 2 · Act on the score

What happens next depends entirely on the total score (see Reading the Score). The score decides whether anything further needs to be charted at all.

Not every screen needs a Communication Book entry. A 0–4 (no depression) result with no provider request is fully handled once the assessment is in the intake form. There is nothing further to chart. The Communication Book comes into play only when the score, item 9, or a provider request means you are notifying the provider.

When the score calls for it · Communication Book

For a positive screen (5+), inform the provider and document in the Communication Book that you notified them:

  1. Go to FORMS → Communication Book.
  2. Select Physician Communication Log.
  3. Enter: Date, Time, Physician Notified, Reason (Other: "Positive Depression Screening"), and Comments if needed.
  4. Click the green check mark to save.

If the patient refuses or is unable to respond

A patient can decline, and some are unable to respond. The measure still needs to know what happened. That's what the exclusion path is for:

  1. Go to Forms → MIPS Quality Exclusions.
  2. Select "Depression Screening."
  3. Document under Adult or Adolescent, as appropriate.
  4. Select the exclusion reason and sign.
  5. No further action is required once this is documented.
Why this counts. When you complete the PHQ accurately and document the handoff cleanly, three things happen at once: patients feel heard and supported, providers get the information they need to act, and our clinic meets the quality standard that keeps this care available. The screen takes a few minutes. For the right patient, on the right day, it can change everything.
Apply ItDolores Hartley, 67
Dolores says, "I really don't want to fill out another questionnaire today. Can we just skip it?"

A patient can decline. The measure still needs to know what happened, respectfully and on the record.

Your turn: Dolores declines the screen. Where do you document this so the visit is handled correctly, and what happens after you do?

A strong answer would include

You document the refusal under Forms → MIPS Quality Exclusions → "Depression Screening," choose Adult (Dolores is 67), select the exclusion reason, and sign. Once that's documented, no further action is required. The visit is correctly accounted for, and Dolores's choice is respected.
