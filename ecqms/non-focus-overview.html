Non-Focus Measures: Overview

Important screenings we monitor year-round, even though they're not our point-earning focus.

These measures aren't our primary point-earning focus this year, but they matter just as much to our patients. They're preventive screenings that catch serious disease early, and we monitor them throughout the year. Your role on these is the same as anywhere else: spot who's due, prompt the care, and document it accurately.

A note on timing. Cancer-screening rates build up over the course of the year as patients come in for visits, so early-year percentages naturally look low and climb as the months go on. Don't be discouraged by a low January number; the goal is steady progress toward benchmark.

The measures we monitor

CMS-139Screening for Future Fall RiskBenchmark 63%

Older adults are screened for their risk of future falls, so the care team can step in before an injury happens. Falls are a leading cause of serious harm in older patients, and a simple screening can flag who needs balance support, a medication review, or a home-safety conversation.

Your role: identify eligible older patients during rooming and tee up the fall-risk screening so it isn't missed.
CMS-124Cervical Cancer ScreeningBenchmark 69.19%

Eligible patients are screened for cervical cancer on the recommended schedule (for example, Pap testing, with HPV testing per guideline age). Regular screening catches changes early, when they're highly treatable.

Your role: recognize when a patient is due, prompt the provider, and make sure results and dates land in the right place in the chart.
CMS-125Breast Cancer ScreeningBenchmark 85.58%

Eligible patients are screened for breast cancer with mammography on the recommended schedule. Early detection dramatically improves outcomes, which is why keeping patients current matters so much.

Your role: flag patients who are due or overdue, help coordinate the mammogram order or referral, and document completion.
CMS-130Colorectal Cancer ScreeningBenchmark 83.54%

Eligible patients are screened for colorectal cancer by one of several accepted methods (for example, colonoscopy on its interval, or a stool-based test on its schedule). It's one of the most preventable cancers when caught early.

Your role: identify who's due, help track stool-test kits sent and returned, and document the screening and date.
