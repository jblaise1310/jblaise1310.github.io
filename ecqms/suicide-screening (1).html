Suicide Screening: C-SSRS

Always completed with the depression screen. Your job: screen, document, and follow provider guidance.
Section 1

Why It Matters

Suicide is often preventable, and the people most at risk rarely say so first. Asking directly opens the door.

988
Suicide & Crisis Lifeline
Free, confidential, and available 24/7. Call or text 988, or chat at 988colorado.com. Anyone can use it, including you.

Why we screen

The C-SSRS is part of our Zero Suicide initiative: the commitment that no patient in our care should fall through the cracks. We screen every patient for suicide risk, and we do it alongside the depression screen, because suicide is a leading cause of death in Colorado and most people at risk are never in mental health care. The visit in front of you may be the only chance to catch it.

Asking does not plant the idea. A common worry is that asking about suicide will put the thought in someone's head. Research is clear that it does not. Asking directly, in a calm and matter-of-fact way, can relieve distress and open the door to help. Your job is to ask the questions exactly as written and let the answers guide what comes next.

Why the point of care matters. Colorado consistently ranks among the ten highest states for suicide. Many people who die had a depressed mood, yet most were not connected to mental health care. Screening at the visit, every time, is how we reach the people a specialty clinic never sees.

Why it is paired with the depression screen

Depression and suicide risk are closely linked, so the C-SSRS is always completed together with the depression (PHQ) screen. A positive depression screen, or a positive answer on PHQ-9 item 9, sits right next to the questions you will ask here. Done together, the two screens give the provider a fuller picture in a single visit.

Section 2

The C-SSRS

A short, standardized set of questions, asked exactly as written. The answers map to a risk level.

What it is

The C-SSRS (Columbia Suicide Severity Rating Scale) is a brief, validated set of yes or no questions that screens for suicide risk. Ask each question exactly as written, and enter the patient's responses exactly as given. The patient can also complete it on paper independently. You are gathering the answers, not judging them.

The questions, and the risk level each can trigger

Question 1 · Low
In the past month, have you wished you were dead or wished you could go to sleep and not wake up?
Question 2 · Low
In the past month, have you actually had thoughts about killing yourself?
Question 3 · Medium
In the past month, have you been thinking about how you might do this?
Question 4 · High
In the past month, have you had these thoughts and had some intention of acting on them?
Question 5 · High
In the past month, have you started to work out or worked out the details of how to do this? Do you intend to carry out this plan?
Question 6a / 6b · Med / Low
Have you ever done anything, started to do anything, or prepared to do anything to end your life? 6a asks about your lifetime; 6b asks about the past 3 months.
Branching logic. If the patient answers yes to Question 2, ask Questions 3, 4, 5, 6a, and 6b. If the patient answers no to Question 2, skip ahead to Question 6a. The screen follows this path on its own; just answer where it takes you.

How the answers escalate

  • Low trigger: Question 1 and/or Question 2 positive.
  • Medium trigger: Question 3 and/or Question 6 positive.
  • High trigger: Question 4, Question 5, and/or recent suicidal behavior positive.

The provider responds to the highest level triggered. You don't calculate this, but it helps to recognize that a yes further down the list means more urgency.

Section 3

Your Role

Screen, document, notify, and follow provider guidance. You do not decide the clinical response.

Step 1 · Screen

Complete the C-SSRS with the patient during the same intake as the depression screen. Ask each question exactly as written and follow the branching logic. The patient can also complete the screen on paper.

Step 2 · Document the screening in the intake form

Document the C-SSRS within your intake form (for example, the Adult Comprehensive Intake), the same way you document the depression screen. This is done for every patient you screen.

Step 3 · Notify the provider

For any positive screen, or if the patient declines or is unable to respond, inform the provider and document that notification in the Communication Book (Reason: Physician Notified), dated to match the encounter.

Step 4 · Follow provider guidance

The clinical response belongs to the provider. Your job is to carry out what the provider directs and keep the patient safe and under our care.

Your follow-up action is simple: follow provider guidance. You are not expected to assess risk level or decide the response. You screen accurately, document, notify the provider, and do what they direct. Understanding the provider actions (next section) helps you support that process and recognize urgency.

If the patient refuses or is unable to respond

Respect the patient's choice, and still capture the attempt. In the C-SSRS Additional Information field, type Refused Screening. That records that the screen was offered and attempted, so the chart can tell a patient who declined from one who was never screened at all.

Section 4

Provider Actions

What happens after a positive screen. You don't run this, but you need to understand it.

You don't determine these actions. Knowing them helps you understand the urgency, support the workflow, and recognize when something needs to move quickly. The provider responds to the highest risk level triggered.

Provider actions by risk level

NegativeQ1, Q2 & Q6 all negative
No further action required.
Low RiskQ1 and/or Q2 positive
Place an RM Mental Health referral. Additional actions, if warranted, per the provider's clinical judgment.
Medium RiskQ3 and/or Q6 positive
Clinician judgment with immediate BH consultation or ED transfer. At minimum: a written, signed Safety Plan plus an RM Mental Health referral. If our BH team is not available, request a Tele-Psych consult (clinic patients are transferred to the ED for Tele-Psych).
High RiskQ4, Q5 & / or recent behavior positive
Clinician judgment with immediate BH consultation or ED transfer. At minimum: a written, signed Safety Plan plus an RM Mental Health referral. If our BH team is not available, request Tele-Psych; clinic patients must be transferred to the ED.
What "Immediate BH Consultation" means. The patient is not discharged home and remains under our care, with possible transfer to the ED when the patient agrees. It does not mean behavioral health must, or will, see the patient immediately. We cannot do anything the patient does not consent to.

A provider's plan may also include a written safety plan, a referral to behavioral health, a higher level of care, and means safety counseling. The specifics are always the provider's decision.

Section 5

Finding It in the Chart

Where the C-SSRS lives in the record, and how to confirm the provider was notified.

To review a charted C-SSRS

  1. Open the Documents component.
  2. Click the row of the most recent intake (for example, Adult Comprehensive Intake).
  3. Scroll down the note to locate the C-SSRS documentation.

To confirm a notification (positive screen or refusal)

  1. Open the Documents component.
  2. Click the row of the Communication Book (the date should match the encounter date).
  3. Read Reason: Physician Notified for the details of the notification.
Why findable matters. A screen only protects a patient if the next person can find it. Clean documentation means whoever opens the chart next sees the screen, the notification, and the plan. That is how a single visit keeps protecting a patient long after they leave the room.
