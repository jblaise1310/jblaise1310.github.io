Module 4: Daily Scheduling Tasks

These are the habits that keep tomorrow's schedule honest, not one-time setup, but something you check every single day.

A schedule that looks full on paper isn't the same as a schedule that's actually accurate. These daily checks are what closes that gap.

No-Show, Cancellation & Reschedule Monitoring

These reports tell you where today's schedule is quietly falling apart, before it becomes tomorrow's problem.

What you're watching for

  • Patients marked as no-show, does the chart reflect that accurately, and does the patient need to be re-scheduled or flagged?
  • Cancellations that opened up a slot, can that slot be filled from a waitlist or a patient who's been asking for something sooner?
  • Reschedules that haven't actually been rebooked yet, don't let a "moved" appointment quietly disappear
Why this matters: an empty slot that never gets noticed is a missed visit for someone else. Catching it same-day is the difference between filling it and losing it.

Daily Schedule Audit

A quick daily sweep of the schedule catches small errors before they turn into a patient showing up to the wrong thing.

What to check

  1. Scan for appointments missing required information, wrong encounter type, missing insurance verification, incomplete PreReg
  2. Look for scheduling errors that don't match provider preferences (see the Provider Preferences reference tool), a procedure booked at the wrong duration, or a visit type a provider doesn't do
  3. Confirm tomorrow's early appointments are fully built, not just pending, so the morning doesn't start behind
  4. Flag anything you can't fix yourself and don't guess, ask before you correct something you're unsure about
When something doesn't look right but you're not sure what the fix should be, that's a good moment to check with Jo or Vicki rather than adjusting it on your own and hoping it's right.

Reading the Daily Provider Staffing Post

Posted to Teams each day, this tells you where every provider actually is today, which matters for routing calls, walk-ins, and patient questions correctly.

You're reading this, not creating it. This post is put together and posted by leadership. Your job is to read it at the start of your shift and use it to route things correctly all day.

What a post looks like

How to read it

  • Floor sections tell you which primary care providers are on-site on which floor today, and how many total. A floor showing "0 Providers" means nobody from that group is in today.
  • Specialist rows show the provider, their specialty, and where they are today, a floor, "Surgery" (off performing a procedure elsewhere), or "Off" (not in at all).
  • The bottom line shows walk-in volume and which room is covering walk-ins that day.
Worth a second look: in the sample above, the header date and the walk-in line at the bottom don't match (07/06 vs. 07/03). If you ever see a mismatch like that on the real post, don't assume it's current, confirm with Jo or Vicki before relying on it to route a call.
