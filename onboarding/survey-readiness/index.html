Chapter 01
§1

Protecting Our Patients: Privacy & Confidentiality

Safeguarding Patient Health Information (PHI) is both a legal mandate under HIPAA and a promise of trust. Even small, overheard details can be a HIPAA concern.

Screens

Angle computer screens away from hallways so passersby can't casually view them.

Lock it

Lock your screen before stepping away, even for a moment.

Paperwork

Secure charts and paperwork in designated locations; never leave them unattended.

Papers

Keep loose papers facedown or in covered bins so details aren't exposed.

“

Before you leave a space, ask yourself: if a surveyor or patient walked by, could they see anything that links health info to a name?

Chapter 02
§2

Maintaining a Safe & Professional Environment

A clean, organized facility prevents accidents, controls infection, and keeps us compliant, from a badge worn correctly to a hallway kept clear.

Badge: above the waist, face up
Sprinkler clearance: 18 inches
Hallway rule: can it roll through?
Workstation

No food or drinks at nurse stations. Keep surfaces clean, personal items in drawers, cords organized.

Hallways

Keep pathways clear. Temporary items go to one side only: "if equipment can't roll through, it doesn't belong there."

Storage

No storage under sinks. Keep 18" clearance from sprinkler heads. Clean and dirty items stay separate.

Infrastructure

Report loose handrails, damaged ceiling tiles, or chipped countertops. Don't wait to be asked.

Chapter 03
§3

Infection Prevention & Control

Simple, consistent actions protect patients, colleagues, and ourselves. These standards are non-negotiable.

Hand hygiene

Sanitizer dispensers must be wall-mounted, kept full, and never expired. Report any that aren't.

Linen

Clean linen is stored in covered containers or closed cabinets. Clean and dirty workflows stay separate.

Supplies

Stored off the floor on solid shelving, no wire bottoms. No expired items in patient care areas.

Utility rooms

A room's designation dictates its contents: clean rooms hold only clean items; dirty rooms hold only soiled items.

Chapter 04
§4

Hazardous Materials & Equipment Safety

Proper handling of waste, chemicals, and specialized equipment prevents injury and keeps us compliant.

Sharps

Wall-mounted, never overfilled. Report any container nearing its fill line.

Biohazard

Containers stay covered and located away from general traffic.

Chemicals

Every container is labeled with product name and warnings, stored apart from clean supplies. Know where SDS live.

Gas cylinders

Chained or secured in approved carts, and labeled "In Use," "Full," or "Empty."

“

Eyewash stations must be clearly marked, unobstructed, and inspected weekly so they're ready the moment they're needed.

§6

Your Voice in Safety: The Duty to Speak Up

If something looks off, say something. No concern is too small.

Equipment

Don't use defective equipment. Report it right away.

Sharps

Report overfilled sharps containers to prevent needlestick injuries.

SDS

Know how to access and use Safety Data Sheets in a chemical exposure.

Anything

Report anything that doesn't seem right, even if you're not sure it matters.
