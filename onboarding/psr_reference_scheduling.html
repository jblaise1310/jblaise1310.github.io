Encounter Types

What each appointment type means, and which ones aren't scheduled through the appointment book at all.

Types you should not use when scheduling

  • Block
  • Integrated BH Visits
  • Saturday Walk In
  • Week Day Walk In
Walk-ins aren't scheduled. Walk-in patients are handled through triage and Lead RN coordination, not through the appointment book.

Scheduling Rules

Clinic-wide rules that apply regardless of which provider you're scheduling for.

Reason for Visit
Follow up
State the actual condition or symptom needing follow-up. If the patient has an acute issue, refer to Triage or the Team Nurse instead of scheduling.
e.g. "FU/Sore Throat," "F/U-Weight Loss Medication"
General Follow Up Do not use
Never a valid reason for visit on its own. We need the actual reason.
Med Refill Do not use
Do not schedule as "Med Refill." Check whether the patient is due for an annual exam and schedule Annual if so. Transfer to the Team Nurse to address the immediate refill need.
Procedures requiring a 60-minute override
All of the following require overriding the 30-minute default and scheduling for 60 minutes:
  • Toenail removal
  • IUD removal
  • IUD insertion
  • IUD removal/reinsertion
  • Nexplanon removal
  • Nexplanon insertion
  • Nexplanon removal/reinsertion
  • Joint Injections
Existing patients, 30-minute PCP visit
Schedule as an existing patient visit with the PCP for 30 minutes:
  • Trigger Point Injections
  • Skin biopsy/shave/excision
New Patients
Schedule per template only.
Annuals
Split the allowed number evenly across AM and PM.
If the provider is on walk-in coverage for half the day, the allowed number of annuals is half the normal amount.
Well Child Check
A pediatric patient does not need an established-patient visit before a well child check.
Well child checks are scheduled as their own appointment type, not as an Annual.
