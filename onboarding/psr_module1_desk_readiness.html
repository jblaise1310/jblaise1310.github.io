Module 1: Desk Readiness

The desk is the first physical space a patient meets. Before a word is spoken, they've already formed an impression of this clinic.

A patient decides how much this clinic will take care of them before they've said a single word — by what they see on your desk.

Cleanliness & Tidiness

A clean desk isn't about appearances for their own sake. It tells a patient that the same attention will carry into their care.

Surfaces & workspace

  • Clear desktop of anything not needed for the current patient interaction
  • Wipe down counters, keyboard, and check-in surfaces regularly through the day
  • Clear the pen cup, sanitizer, and tissue box, not empty, not overflowing
  • No food or drink visible in the patient-facing area

Personal items

  • Personal belongings (bags, phones, snacks) stored out of patient view
  • Personal photos or décor kept minimal and professional
Why it matters: in a small clinic, patients often know the staff personally. A tidy desk keeps the interaction feeling professional even when the relationship is familiar.

Professional Appearance

This is about how the space presents itself, not a dress code. What's taped up, posted, or left in view says something about how the clinic runs.

What belongs at the desk

  • Only current, approved signage or notices, nothing outdated or handwritten as a substitute
  • Postings hung straight, not curling, faded, or taped over older versions
  • Patient-facing forms and materials neatly stacked, not crooked or overflowing a tray

What doesn't

  • Sticky notes as a stand-in for a real process (a real note-to-self is fine tucked away, not left facing the patient)
  • Anything with another patient's name or information left visible
Privacy note: a visible sticky note, printout, or open screen with another patient's name on it is a confidentiality issue, not just a tidiness one. Treat "nothing visible" as a privacy rule first.

Opening Duties

The first patient of the day shouldn't be able to tell whether the clinic just opened or has been running for hours.

  • Power on and log into all workstations and iPads before the first scheduled patient
  • Confirm the desk is stocked, clean, and clear from the prior day's closing (or catch anything that was missed)
  • Confirm phones are live and routing correctly
  • Do a quick visual sweep of the waiting area, not just your own desk

Closing Duties

Closing well is a gift to whoever opens tomorrow, and to the first patient who walks in.

  • Shut down all computers properly, don't just leave them logged in
  • Secure iPads per clinic protocol
  • Clear the desk surface, nothing patient-identifiable left out overnight
  • Restock anything that ran low so tomorrow's opener isn't starting behind
Think of it as a handoff: closing duties aren't just about ending your shift, they're about setting up whoever opens next, even if that's you tomorrow.
