March 2026 had only 10 working days, yet McIntyre saw 170 patients at 17.0 visits/day — the highest rate of any month across both years. After-hours EHR spiked to 21.6%, also the highest reading on record. This combination — compressed schedule, maximum intensity, highest after-hours burden — is a clear signal. If it was a one-off, the data tells a very different story.
↑ After-hours % is structurally elevated and trending up
Excluding the March outlier, after-hours EHR has ranged 6–14% across 8 months with a clear upward trend line. The Q1 2026 average (12.5%) is higher than Q4 2025 (10.1%). Panel growth — +70% in 9 months — is the most likely driver. More patients means more inbox, more refills, more results to review after hours. Without a documentation intervention (AI scribe, protected time, or panel stabilization), this number will continue climbing as the panel approaches 900+.
▲ Panel approaching inflection point — planning conversation needed now
At the current growth rate of approximately +35 patients per month, McIntyre's panel will cross 800 in May 2026 and reach approximately 975 by October 2026. For a provider running 30-minute appointment slots, a panel above 900–950 typically requires shorter slot times, extended hours, or active panel management. The July 15 next available appointment date confirms demand is already exceeding supply. Planning conversations need to start now, not when the panel is already there.

Message center usage has been consistently low and stable — averaging 8.1% in Q1 2026, ranging 6.8–9.1%, and well below peers like Nobles (17%) and Mashburn (21%). The AI documentation coaching and workflow changes from 2025 appear to be holding. This is a metric where McIntyre is genuinely efficient and should be explicitly acknowledged during the interview — providers notice when the data shows what they're doing well.
Conversation guide — suggested questions based on data
Use these as starting points — each is grounded in a specific data pattern:
1
March event
"March showed your highest visits-per-day but only 10 working days — what was going on that month? Did you feel it afterward?" Listen for whether this was covering absences, a scheduling anomaly, or self-imposed catch-up. The answer changes how you interpret the after-hours spike.
2
After-hours trend
"Your after-hours EHR time has been around 10–14% most months and climbed to over 21% in March. Where does most of that time go — inbox, results review, refills, or charting?" This helps identify whether the intervention is workflow, staffing, or documentation tooling.
3
Panel planning
"Your panel has grown from 449 to 764 in 9 months and is on track to hit around 940 by September. At what number does it start feeling unmanageable — do you have a sense of where your ceiling is?" Invite her to name the number. It anchors the planning conversation.
4
Access / wait time
"You're booked out to July 15 — nearly two months. How are you handling urgent or same-day requests? Is that creating friction for you or for patients?" This connects access to her daily experience, not just a scheduling metric.
5
AI tools follow-up
"You were using Freed and Dragon last year and it seemed to help. Has that continued? Do you think it's actually reduced your after-hours time?" This also helps clarify the EHR doc time measurement discrepancy — her answer will tell you whether the 2026 numbers reflect real improvement or a changed metric.
6
Open items from 2025
"Phone follow-up visits and portal message billing were on your list last year — did either of those move forward? What's the status?" Closing the loop on prior action items signals that the 1:1 program drives real change, not just conversation.
