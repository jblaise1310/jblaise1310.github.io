Component 4

Meds / Allergies

Two lists in one component: the patient’s allergies and their home-medication history — reviewed, added with generic terms, and marked reviewed.

Know your lane
MAs document medication history only. Never delete, discontinue, cancel, or change a prescribed medication — that’s the physician’s medication reconciliation. On a prescribed med you document compliance only.

Allergies first. With the Display set to Active, you review the list with Mary. “Any allergies we should know about?” She has a few:

  • Penicillin — a drug allergy; gives her hives.
  • Shellfish — a food allergy; causes swelling.
  • Pollen — an environmental allergy; seasonal congestion and itchy eyes.

For each one you click +Add, search the substance, pick the most generic term from the binoculars list, set the Category (drug, food, environmental), and save with OK. You leave Severity blank — it’s only entered for anaphylaxis — and record each reaction. Since she does have known allergies, you don’t click No Known Allergies.

Then medications. Her hypertension is managed with lisinopril, which appears with a pill-bottle icon — a prescribed medication. You don’t touch the dose or anything else; you only document compliance: she confirms she’s Still taking, as prescribed. You click Document Medication by Hx, update compliance, and click Document History.

With both lists confirmed, you click Mark All as Reviewed — once for allergies, once for medications.

Do this · part 1

How to document — Allergies

  • Display setting
    Set Display to Active for this section.
  • No known allergies
    • No Known Allergies — for non-medication allergies (does not include medications).
    • No Known Medication Allergies — medications only.
  • Add an allergy
    • Click +Add, type the allergen, click the binoculars, and select the most generic option.
    • Click OK to save — the yellow requirement clears from the Substance field.
  • Fields to document
    • Substance — do not use the free-text option.
    • Reactions — avoid free text unless absolutely necessary.
    • Severity — only enter if Anaphylaxis; do not enter any other option.
    • Info Source, Category (drug / food / environmental), and a Comment if needed (via the Add Comment button).
  • Modify / statusLimits apply
    Right-click the row → Modify. Active is the default; Proposed is not used; Resolved is provider-only; use Cancelled with an appropriate reason.
  • Mark All as Reviewed
    Click once the allergy list is confirmed with the patient.
Do this · part 2

How to document — Medication History

History only. Document the home-med history and compliance — never delete, discontinue, cancel, or change a prescribed medication.
  • Document Medication by Hx
    Click to adjust medications as appropriate. Prescribed meds (pill-bottle icon) — document compliance only; make no other changes.
  • No meds / unable to obtain
    • Not taking any home meds → No Known Home Medications.
    • Can’t obtain the history → Unable to Obtain Information.
  • Add a medication
    Click +Add → Common Hx Medications folder → select the most generic form; if multiple doses, select the highest listed. Add all, then Done.
  • Compliance
    Right-click the med → Add/Modify Compliance. On the Details tab document Dose, Route, Frequency, Duration if provided. On the Compliance tab set Status (e.g. Still taking, as prescribed). Click Document History when done.
  • Dose change
    New dose → add the medication with the new dose/route/frequency. Same med, current → update compliance from the patient’s report.
  • Mark All as Reviewed
    Click once the medication list is confirmed.
Why it matters

Function of the component

  • Maintains an accurate allergy list — driving interaction and allergy checking for safe prescribing.
  • Records the patient’s home-medication history and compliance for the provider’s reconciliation.
  • Clear roles: MA documents history; the physician reconciles.
Good habits

Tips

  • Always pick the most generic allergen and medication terms — and avoid free text.
  • Severity is for anaphylaxis only; leave it blank otherwise.
  • On a prescribed med, document compliance only — never change dose, route, or status.
  • Use Non-Formulary Patient’s Own Med when a home medication can’t be found in the system.
  • Add to Favorites (with your own folder) to speed up frequently entered allergens and meds.
