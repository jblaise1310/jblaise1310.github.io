You review the visit summary fields with Mary. “What brings you in today?” you ask. She smiles, “Just my yearly checkup — nothing new going on.” You enter Annual Wellness Exam as her Chief Complaint, with nothing to add under Onset of Symptoms. She’s been postmenopausal for several years, so you record that for LMP and Menstrual Status.

At the scale (the Measurements section, which feeds the BMI measure) you record 182 lbs and a height of 5′6″. “Same as last year — must be all my morning walks with Daisy,” she says. BMI auto-calculates. In the Vitals section you carry over the check-in vitals: BP 148/92 mmHg, HR 78 bpm, and Temp 98.6°F.

The pain screen. Mary said nothing was new, so you almost expect this to be quick — but when you ask, she pauses: “Well… my lower back has been bothering me. I didn’t think it counted — it’s been a few months.” She rates it an 8. Because pain is present, you document the whole picture: Pain Present Yes, Pain (0–10) 8, Primary Pain Location Back lower, and Pain Comments patient reports pain ongoing for last 4 months. It stays in the pain screen — not her Chief Complaint — unless the provider directs otherwise.

Respiratory. Mary is breathing comfortably: Rate 18 br/min, SpO2 % 97%. She’s on no supplemental oxygen, so you select Room air for Oxygen Therapy and leave Oxygen Flow Rate blank.

Do this

How to document

Chief complaint & history
  • Chief Complaint
    • Enter on one line with no hard returns.
    • For an AWV, document Annual Wellness Exam unless an additional reason for visit is reported.
  • Onset of Symptoms
    Document as applicable.
  • LMP / Menstrual Status
    Complete based on the patient’s report.
MeasurementsBMI Quality Measure
  • Weight & Height
    • Enter measured weight and height in both fields available.
    • Use “Patient stated height/weight” in comments only if not measured.
    • Verify BMI auto-populates.
VitalsBlood pressure & generalHypertension Quality Measure
  • Blood Pressure
    Enter SBP / DBP and BP Site. SBP/DBP entry is required for the hypertension measure.
  • Heart Rate & Temperature
    Record HR and Temp. (Respiratory rate and SpO₂ are entered in the Respiratory area below.)
Vitals — PainIf pain → all fields
If the patient reports pain, every pain field is required. A “yes” with no score, location, or comment is incomplete — this is the most common miss.
  • Pain Present
    Select from the dropdown — e.g. Yes — actual or suspected pain, or the appropriate no-pain option. This answer drives everything below.
  • Pain (0–10)Required if pain
    Enter the patient’s own rating, 0 to 10. Use their number.
  • Primary Pain LocationRequired if pain
    Choose from the dropdown (e.g. Back lower). If pain is present, this cannot be blank.
  • Pain CommentsRequired if pain
    Free text for onset, duration, quality — e.g. patient reports pain ongoing for last 4 months.
Vitals — RespiratoryIf O₂ → all fields
If the patient is on any supplemental oxygen, complete all respiratory fields. This builds the oxygen-needs history a pulmonary specialist relies on at referral.
  • Rate
    Respiratory rate in br/min.
  • SpO2 %
    Oxygen saturation as a percentage.
  • Oxygen Flow RateRequired if on O₂
    Flow in L/min. Complete whenever the patient is on supplemental oxygen; leave blank for room air.
  • Oxygen TherapyAlways select one
    Check the delivery method — select Room air if the patient is on none; don’t leave it blank. The list is long and scrolls (nasal cannula, masks, high-flow, CPAP/BiPAP, trach, ventilator, and more) — pick the exact device.
  • Temperature Calculator
    A helper to convert °F if needed — the temperature itself is recorded with the general vitals above.
Why & what it feeds

Function of the component & quality measures

  • Provides a concise summary of the visit purpose and relevant background.
  • BMI Quality Measure. Documents height and weight to calculate BMI for quality reporting.
  • Hypertension Quality Measure. Documents BP for quality reporting and triggers alerts if elevated.
  • Pain. A complete pain entry (score + location + comments) supports pain-management decisions, follow-up, and referral — and a partial entry breaks the trend over time.
  • Respiratory & oxygen. Builds a documented history of the patient’s oxygen needs — essential for a pulmonary-specialist referral. SpO₂, flow rate, and device together describe how much support the patient needs.

If the patient declines the scale or height measurement: document what they report in the weight fields and enter a comment of Patient Stated. Document Weight, Height/Length, and Weight (Dosing).

Second set of vitals? Document the first set here. If a second assessment is required, document it on the Ambulatory Vitals Height Weight form.
Good habits

Tips

  • Keep acute issues out of the Chief Complaint for an Annual Wellness Exam unless the provider directs you to.
  • Enter both height and weight in the correct fields to trigger the BMI calculation.
  • SBP/DBP entry is required for the hypertension measure — ensure accuracy.
  • Pain: ask directly even at a wellness visit (patients often don’t volunteer chronic pain), record the patient’s own 0–10 number, and put onset/duration in Pain Comments. Don’t stop at “yes.”
  • Respiratory: document Room air too — a blank field reads as “not assessed,” not “no oxygen.” On oxygen, record the flow rate and the exact device.
  • Use the calculator tools for imperial/metric or °F conversions.
