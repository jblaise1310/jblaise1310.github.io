Component 6

Procedures & Surgeries

The patient’s surgical history — each procedure with a name, a date (exact or approximate), and any clarifying comment.

Scenario

Mary’s visit, continued

Procedure 1
Cholecystectomy · age 42
Procedure 2
Cataract surgery, right eye · last year
Procedure 3
Mole removal, arm · approx.
Closeout
Mark All as Reviewed

“Any surgeries or procedures I should add?” you ask. Mary recalls, “Gallbladder out when I was 42, cataract surgery on my right eye last year, and a mole removed from my arm — don’t remember the date.”

You enter each one. For the gallbladder and cataract you have datable references; for the mole removal you note an Approximate Date and flag it as approximate in the comments. The cataract on her right eye gets a laterality comment — and it lines up with the reading-glasses note from her vision screen.

Once the list is confirmed with Mary, you click Mark All as Reviewed.

Do this

How to document

Right section only. Chart in the Procedures / Surgical History section — nowhere else.
  • Name
    Enter the name of the surgery or procedure, using standardized terms.
  • Date
    Enter the date — or an Approximate Date if the exact date is unknown.
  • Comments
    Add clarification if needed — e.g. laterality (right/left) or complications.
  • Mark All as Reviewed
    Click once the list is confirmed with the patient.
Why it matters

Function of the component

  • Maintains an accurate historical record of significant surgical procedures.
  • Helps the provider identify risk factors, relevant history, and potential complications.
Good habits

Tips

  • Always confirm the spelling of procedure names (use standardized terms).
  • Unknown date? Enter an estimated year and note in Comments that it’s approximate.
  • Review and update at each visit to keep the history accurate.
