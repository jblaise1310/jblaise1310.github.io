Intake Documentation: Practice Scenarios

Three adult (18+) encounters to practice charting a complete intake in Cerner. Each patient has real updates to chart, so your documentation can actually be assessed. Watch for changes (flagged new), screening results, and refusals.

How to use these. Read each scenario, then chart the encounter in Cerner exactly as you would for a real patient. Add and update what has changed, complete every required component, correctly handle screenings (including a refusal and a positive result), and skip only what truly doesn't apply. Record the FIN, then use Check your documentation to compare against the model.
Required components for a complete adult intake
Summary (CC, vitals, measurements) ID Risk Screen Problems & Medical Hx Meds & Allergies Hearing & Vision Procedures & Surgeries Family Hx Social Hx Advance Directive* STEADI Fall Risk* Depression Screening C-SSRS
*Conditional. STEADI: every visit for patients 65+. Advance Directive: patients 65+ at their annual exam. Always confirm whether a component truly applies to the patient in front of you.
Scenario 1
Harold Whitman, 71
Visit type: routine blood pressure follow-up (not an annual exam)
Foundational

Harold is an established 71-year-old here to follow up on his blood pressure. He's in good spirits and has a few updates since his last visit. "My knee's been bothering me, the doctor I saw said it's arthritis in both knees. And my heart doctor started me on a new pill, metoprolol I think. Oh, and I finally had that cataract surgery on my right eye a couple months back, seeing much better now. Quit the cigarettes too, about three months ago." He also mentions his younger brother was just diagnosed with colon cancer. He denies pain today, no recent travel, and voices no mood concerns.

Vitals & measurements
BP: 138/82, left arm
HR: 74, radial
Temp: 98.2°F, temporal
RR: 16
SpO2: 97%
O2: room air
Weight: 189 lb
Height: 5'10"
Pain: denies
Age: 71
History updates (per Harold)
New problem: osteoarthritis, both knees new
New medication: metoprolol, started by cardiologist new
New surgery: right cataract surgery, ~2 months ago new
Family Hx: brother recently diagnosed with colon cancer new
Social Hx: quit smoking ~3 months ago (former smoker) change
Allergies: unchanged (NKDA)
Travel: none, self or household
Hearing & Vision: vision improved post-cataract; no concerns
Mood: no concerns voiced
Chart a complete intake in Cerner. Add and update everything that has changed, then decide carefully which conditional items his age and visit type do, and do not, require.

Social Hx: update tobacco to former smoker, quit ~3 mo ago; review alcohol, vaping, substance use; Mark All as Reviewed.
STEADI Fall Risk: required, Harold is 65+ and STEADI is done every visit.
Depression Screening (18+): complete; PHQ-2 negative, no concerns.
C-SSRS: complete (every patient, every visit), negative.
Advance Directive: NOT required today.
The trick: Harold is 71, so it's tempting to complete the Advance Directive, but it's only required for 65+ patients at their annual exam. This is a routine BP follow-up, not an annual, so Advance Directive is not required. STEADI is still required because it's done every visit for 65+. Same patient, two different answers.
Scenario 2
Daniela Reyes, 29
Visit type: sick visit, sore throat and ear pain
Building

Daniela is a 29-year-old with a sore throat and ear pain that started three days ago. "It started Saturday and it's gotten worse, the right side especially. It hurts to swallow." She rates her throat pain 6/10 on the right. She returned eight days ago from a 10-day trip visiting family in Mexico, and she just started a new over-the-counter allergy pill (cetirizine). When you begin the depression screening, she stops you: "Honestly, I'd rather not do those questionnaires today, I'm just here for my throat." She declines both the depression screening and the suicide-risk questions. Her problems, surgeries, family history, and social history are unchanged, and she has no hearing or vision concerns.

Vitals & measurements
BP: 118/76, right arm
HR: 88, radial
Temp: 100.6°F, temporal
RR: 18
SpO2: 99%
O2: room air
Weight: 141 lb
Height: 5'4"
Pain: 6/10, right throat
Age: 29
History updates (per Daniela)
New medication: cetirizine (OTC allergy pill), just started new
Travel: returned 8 days ago from a 10-day trip to Mexico flag
Problems / Surgeries / Family / Social: unchanged
Hearing & Vision: no concerns
Screening
Depression screening: patient declined
C-SSRS (suicide risk): patient declined
Chart a complete intake in Cerner. Handle the travel, the new medication, and especially the refused screenings correctly, how do you document a screening the patient declined?

Complete intake, component by component
Summary: CC = sore throat / ear pain, with onset (3 days ago / Saturday). Full measurements; temp 100.6 (elevated). Pain present, document score (6/10) and location (right throat).
ID Risk Screen: recent travel yes; trip was 10 days (7+), so the travel location is required (Mexico).
Problems & Medical Hx: reviewed, Mark All as Reviewed.
Meds & Allergies: add cetirizine (OTC); Mark All as Reviewed.
Hearing & Vision: no concerns.
Procedures & Surgeries: reviewed, Mark All as Reviewed.
Family Hx: unchanged, confirm at top.
Social Hx: reviewed, Mark All as Reviewed.
Depression Screening: do NOT leave blank and do NOT mark it negative. Chart that the patient declined / refused the screening, using the refusal option in the form per RHC workflow.
C-SSRS: same, document patient declined / refused; do not record a false negative.
STEADI / Advance Directive: not required, she's 29.
The teaching point: a refused screening is charted as a refusal, never as "negative" and never left blank, marking it negative would put false information in the record. Follow RHC protocol for notifying the provider that the screenings were declined. Also note: 7+ day travel makes the travel location mandatory, and the new OTC allergy pill must be added to the med list.
Scenario 3
Marcus Bell, 54
Visit type: diabetes & hypertension follow-up
Challenge

Marcus is a 54-year-old with diabetes and hypertension here for follow-up. He's quieter than usual. "Honestly, not great. I haven't had energy and I haven't cared much about anything lately." He confirms his medications but says he ran out of his lisinopril about a week ago. He had a sleep study last month and was told he has sleep apnea, and he mentions his father had a heart attack at 60. He denies physical pain and no recent travel. On the depression screening his responses are positive, and on the suicide-risk screen he answers that he has wished he were dead in the past month, but reports no thoughts of killing himself, no plan, no intent, and no prior attempt.

Vitals & measurements
BP: 152/94, left arm
HR: 80, radial
Temp: 98.0°F, temporal
RR: 16
SpO2: 98%
O2: room air
Weight: 214 lb
Height: 5'11"
Pain: denies
Age: 54
History updates (per Marcus)
New problem: obstructive sleep apnea, dx by recent sleep study new
Medication: ran out of lisinopril ~1 week ago reconcile
Family Hx: father, heart attack at age 60 new
Surgeries / Social: unchanged
Travel: none, self or household
Hearing & Vision: no concerns
Screening results
PHQ-2: positive (proceeds to PHQ-9)
PHQ-9 total: 12, moderate; item 9 (better off dead) endorsed "several days"
C-SSRS: wished dead in past month = yes; thoughts of killing self = no; no plan, intent, or prior attempt → low risk, but positive
Chart a complete intake in Cerner. Document the updates and the screening results accurately, and decide what a positive screen requires of you, and where your role ends and the provider's begins.

The teaching point, do they escalate? A positive depression screen and any positive C-SSRS response must be communicated to the provider promptly, before the patient leaves, per RHC protocol. You chart the results exactly as the patient reported them and follow the screener's triage workflow. You do not interpret, diagnose, or reassure the patient that it's "nothing", that's the provider's role. Accurate documentation + prompt escalation is the job.
