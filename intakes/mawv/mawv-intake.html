Purpose of this guide

These sheets walk you through what to enter, where, and why for the components completed at a Medicare AWV. Each component is its own page with the same four parts: a short scenario with our patient Ray, a How to Document checklist, the function of the component (including any quality measures it feeds), and quick tips. Use them at the elbow while you chart, or as a refresher before a shift.

The AWV is a prevention and screening visit, not a head-to-toe exam, so the real skill is knowing which components apply. This guide covers the ones documented for Ray; the others are completed only when indicated, and each Tips section notes when they apply.

Why it matters

Why accurate documentation matters

Quality measures. Fields like BMI, blood pressure, and depression screening feed reportable quality measures. If they aren't captured in the right place, the measure isn't met, even if the care happened.

Patient safety & continuity. The provider and the next visit rely on what you entered. Clean, complete data prevents missed history, duplicated tests, and medication errors.

Billing & compliance. Documentation supports the level of service billed and stands as the legal record of the visit. Consistent entry keeps the practice audit-ready.

One source of truth. Entering data in the field Cerner expects, not just in a comment, means it flows to flowsheets, trends, and downstream forms automatically.

1

Summary

BMI · HTN

Scenario

Ray comes back for his yearly Medicare wellness visit. The MA confirms two identifiers, then asks what brings him in. "Just my yearly Medicare check-up, the wellness one."

How to Document

  • Reason for visit: Medicare Annual Wellness Visit
  • Vitals: BP 142/84 (right arm, seated), Pulse 72, Resp 16, Temp 98.2 °F oral, SpO2 97% room air
  • Measurements: Height 70 in (measured today), Weight 198 lb, BMI 28.4 (auto-calculates)

What this component does

The Summary anchors the encounter. Reason for visit marks it as preventive and drives the AWV template and the screenings that follow. BMI and blood pressure entered here feed reportable quality measures.

Tips

  • Chart vitals as measured. Don't round an elevated BP down to normal; recheck per protocol and chart both.
  • Measure height and weight today so BMI is real, not carried forward.
  • Flag an elevated reading to the provider per protocol. Don't interpret or counsel.

Scenario

How to Document

  • Patient Self-Assessment: Good
  • Patient Counseled: leave for whoever delivers the counseling
  • Care Providers: Reyes, Alan · Orthopedics · right knee OA
  • Ancillary: San Juan Physical Therapy · PT · Pagosa Springs
  • Medical Devices: None · DME: CPAP
  • Oral Health: assessment Yes; dental exam past 12 mo No; concern: chipped/missing teeth; comment: two missing lower molars, denies pain

What this component does

Captures the patient's own health rating, current care team, equipment, and oral-health status, all of which the provider and downstream forms rely on.

Tips

  • Patient Counseled is checked by the person who delivers counseling, not pre-checked at rooming.
  • Enter only real providers and devices the patient reports. A blank field beats a guess.

ID Risk Screen

Scenario

The MA asks the two travel questions. "I haven't been anywhere myself. My son just got back from Italy a couple weeks ago, though, stayed with us a few nights."

How to Document

  • Patient travel: No recent travel
  • Family / close-contact travel: Yes, son traveled to Italy within the last 2 weeks

Positive close-contact travel: notify the provider per the exposure protocol

What this component does

Flags infectious-disease exposure risk so the team can mask, room, and notify appropriately.

Tips

  • Ask both questions and chart the real answers. A negative you didn't ask for is not a screen.
  • If either answer is yes, document it and follow the exposure protocol to flag the provider.

Advance Directive

Required

Scenario

The MA asks whether Ray has an advance directive and whether a copy is on file. "We talked about it, but never put anything on paper."

How to Document

  • Has an advance directive: No
  • Copy on file / provided today: No
  • Would like information: Yes
  • Healthcare agent designated: No

What this component does

Required for patients 65+ at the annual exam, which the AWV is. Captures whether the patient's wishes are documented and on file.

Tips

  • Patients 65+ get this at an annual exam, which the AWV qualifies as. STEADI, by contrast, is done at every visit for 65+. Know the difference so you complete the right ones.
  • Required (yellow) fields are never blank. If truly unknown, choose Unknown, after asking.
  • Never chart a directive as "on file" if it isn't. Offer the info packet; the planning conversation is provider-led.

Problems and Medical History

Scenario

The MA reads the problem list to Ray, confirms it, and asks what's new. "That's all right. Only new thing, I've been getting heartburn a few nights a week."

How to Document

  • Reviewed: HTN, hyperlipidemia, right knee OA, OSA (CPAP), confirmed active
  • New reported concern: intermittent heartburn, several nights/week, as a symptom for provider evaluation

What this component does

Keeps the active problem list accurate for the provider and for continuity of care.

Tips

  • "Reviewed" is earned by reading the list aloud and confirming, never a stamp.
  • Chart a new complaint as a symptom, don't add a diagnosis. New diagnoses belong to the provider.

7

Meds / Allergies

Scenario

The MA documents Ray's allergy history and his full home-medication list, asking specifically about OTCs, supplements, and the recent eye drops. "I take an omeprazole most mornings, vitamin D and fish oil. I'll be honest, I skip the cholesterol pill a couple times a week, my legs ache."

How to Document

  • Allergies: sulfa (rash); codeine (nausea/vomiting)
  • Lisinopril 20 mg PO daily
  • Atorvastatin 40 mg PO daily, reports skipping ~2x/week (leg aches)
  • Acetaminophen 500 mg PO PRN (knee)
  • Omeprazole 20 mg OTC most mornings (new); Vitamin D, fish oil (new)
  • Eye drops: completed, none current

What this component does

A clean medication and allergy record prevents interactions and errors and informs every prescribing decision. The MA documents the history; the provider reconciles it. No orders are changed at intake.

Tips

  • Ask about OTCs, supplements, PRNs, and eye drops by name; they only surface when you ask.
  • Document non-adherence honestly. "As prescribed" would hide what the provider needs.
  • Chart the reaction described; let the provider classify allergy vs intolerance. Re-ask directly; never stamp NKDA you didn't confirm.
  • Scope: document the history and flag concerns. The MA does not reconcile, adjust doses, or start and stop medications. That is the provider's role.

Family History

Required

Scenario

The MA updates family history with Ray. "My brother just got diagnosed with colon cancer this spring."

How to Document

  • Father: deceased, myocardial infarction (age 68)
  • Mother: deceased, type 2 diabetes
  • Brother: hypertension; new: colon cancer, diagnosed this year
  • Children (2): alive and well

What this component does

Captures hereditary risk that can change screening recommendations.

Tips

  • Specificity matters: relationship, condition, age at diagnosis or death.
  • Surface a new cancer diagnosis; the provider decides on screening changes.
  • Unknown is an honest answer; a blank is not. Required field.

Social History

Scenario

The MA works through each Social History category with Ray: alcohol, tobacco, vaping, and substance use. "Quit smoking about 15 years ago, smoked a fair bit before. Couple beers on the weekend. Never touched the vapes or anything else."

How to Document

  • Alcohol: 1-2 beers on weekends (must match the Alcohol Screening)
  • Tobacco: former smoker, quit approx. 15 years ago, approx. 30 pack-year history
  • Electronic Cigarette/Vaping: denies, never used
  • Substance Use: denies

What this component does

Captures behavioral and social factors. Tobacco status feeds screening eligibility (lung cancer, AAA) and quality reporting.

Tips

  • A former smoker is charted as former, with quit date and pack-years, never "non-smoker."
  • Substance Use is required (red marker). Chart "denies" rather than leaving it blank.
  • The Alcohol entry here must match the Alcohol Screening component. Same patient, same number.

STEADI Fall Risk Assessment

Positive

Scenario

The MA asks the three STEADI key questions. "Went down once, about four months back, no injury. The knee makes me unsteady sometimes, and I think about it more than I used to."

How to Document

  • Fallen in past year: Yes, 1 fall ~4 months ago, no injury
  • Feels unsteady: Yes, intermittent (knee)
  • Worries about falling: Yes

Result: positive screen, increased fall risk

What this component does

Screens fall risk at every 65+ visit. A positive screen drives further assessment and intervention.

Tips

  • Chart exactly what he reports: one fall, no injury.
  • A positive screen means the provider is made aware before the patient leaves.
  • Scoring the tool is documentation, within scope. The clinical response is the provider's.

Depression Screening

Depression

Scenario

The PHQ-2 and full PHQ-9 are completed, sometimes read aloud by the MA, sometimes filled out by Ray on paper. Either way, the MA charts the results into Cerner.

How to Document

  • All nine PHQ-9 item scores entered (0-3 each)
  • PHQ-2: 0 (negative)
  • PHQ-9 total: 2 (minimal)
  • Item 9: 0 (negative)

What this component does

Screens for depression and feeds the depression-screening quality measure. A complete screen is the PHQ-2 and the full PHQ-9, both documented.

Tips

  • Score every item; don't summarize the mood. The total is computed from the answers.
  • Item 9 is the trip-wire, independent of the total. Any positive triggers the suicide screen and immediate escalation before the patient leaves.

12

C-SSRS Screen, With Triage, Outpatient

Scenario

The C-SSRS is completed exactly as written, sometimes read aloud by the MA, sometimes filled out by Ray on paper. The MA charts the results into Cerner.

How to Document

  • Past month, wished you were dead / to not wake up: No
  • Past month, thoughts of killing yourself: No
  • Plan / intent / preparation (Q3-5): not administered (gated off by No to Q2)
  • Lifetime behavior: No

Triage: negative / low, no acute intervention indicated

What this component does

A structured suicide-risk screen with built-in triage that maps the risk level to a required action. At our clinic it is part of the Zero Suicide initiative, our commitment to systematically identify and support anyone at risk, so it is completed on every patient, not only when the depression screen is positive.

Tips

  • Screened universally under Zero Suicide, completed for every patient regardless of the PHQ-9 result.
  • Read as written, in order. The tool's logic decides which follow-ups open. Don't soften the wording.
  • Document the complete negative plus the triage result.
  • A positive screen means the patient does not leave without provider evaluation.
  • Tone is part of the skill; calm and non-judgmental yields honest answers.

Hearing and Vision Screening

Scenario

The MA asks about vision, then hearing. "Eyes are good since the cataract surgery, just readers. Hearing's another story, I'm always asking Donna to repeat herself."

How to Document

  • Vision: no concern, good since cataract surgery, uses reading glasses
  • Hearing: concern present, difficulty in noisy settings, asks others to repeat

What this component does

Patient-reported functional vision and hearing status. A reported concern may prompt a referral.

Tips

  • Patient-reported. Chart what he reports, not the convenient "no concerns" default.
  • Surface a reported concern; don't diagnose hearing loss or call it "just age."

Functional Assessment

Scenario

The MA asks how Ray manages daily self-care. "I handle all of it. Only thing, getting in and out of the tub is tricky with the knee."

How to Document

  • Bathing: independent, difficulty in/out of tub (knee)
  • Dressing, toileting, transferring, feeding: independent · Continence: continent
  • Overall: independent in ADLs, with noted bathing difficulty

What this component does

Captures basic activities of daily living (self-care), distinct from the IADL component.

Tips

  • Capture the one real exception. The tub difficulty completes the fall-risk picture with STEADI and the grab bars.
  • Self-care is ADL here; managing money, meds, and transportation is IADL.

Alcohol Screening

Scenario

The MA runs the alcohol screen, keeping it consistent with what was charted in Social History.

How to Document (AUDIT-C)

  • Frequency: 2-4 times a month (2)
  • Typical drinks per day: 1-2 (0)
  • 6 or more on one occasion: never (0)

Total: 2, below the at-risk threshold (men ≥4)

What this component does

Screens for unhealthy alcohol use.

Tips

  • The number here must match Social History. A chart that disagrees with itself isn't trustworthy.
  • Score it and document it, even when negative.
