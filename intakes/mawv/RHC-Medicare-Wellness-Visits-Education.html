Knowing Your Medicare Wellness Visits

Three different visits, three different rules. Before you room a Medicare patient, know which one they are here for and why, because it shapes what you document.

Why it matters

Why the visit type matters

In Cerner there is a single MAWV form used for all of these visits, so the form itself does not change. What changes is the patient's eligibility, where they are in their Medicare journey, and what the provider focuses on. Knowing which visit your patient is here for is foundational understanding that helps you room them well, and most new MAs have never been taught it.

  • The type tells you where the patient is in their Medicare journey. Each visit has its own eligibility window.
  • The type shapes the work. A first visit establishes the baseline; a yearly visit updates it.
  • None of them is a head-to-toe physical exam. Depending on the issue, the provider may also address a problem during the visit.

The sequence over time

Most patients move through these in order, anchored to when their Medicare Part B coverage started.

Updates the baseline. Once per 12-month period.

IPPE

"Welcome to Medicare" preventive visit

When

First 12 months after Part B starts

How often

Once per lifetime

What it includes

  • Review of medical and social history, including diet, activity, and tobacco, alcohol, and drug use
  • Depression risk and functional ability and safety review
  • A limited exam: height, weight, BMI, blood pressure, balance, gait, and a visual acuity screen
  • End-of-life planning, if the patient agrees
  • Opioid and substance-use review, education, counseling, and referrals
  • A written checklist of preventive services, including a referral for a once-in-a-lifetime screening EKG when appropriate
MA noteThis is the only Medicare wellness visit with hands-on exam pieces (vision, balance, gait). It is tied to the first 12 months of Part B. If the patient is past that window, they are no longer eligible for an IPPE and the visit becomes an AWV.

First AWV

Initial Annual Wellness Visit

When

After 12 months of Part B, and not within 12 months of an IPPE

How often

Once per lifetime

What it includes (establishes the baseline)

  • A Health Risk Assessment (HRA)
  • Establishes medical and family history, a current providers and suppliers list, a written screening schedule, and a list of risk factors and conditions
  • Measures height, weight, BMI, and blood pressure
  • Cognitive impairment is checked by the provider (Mini-Cog when applicable); depression risk and functional ability and safety are reviewed
  • Personalized health advice and referrals; advance care planning at the patient's discretion
MA noteThis is where the prevention baseline is built, so the history, providers, and screening schedule are established for the first time. It is not a physical exam, and there is no required visual acuity or balance and gait exam like the IPPE has.

Subsequent AWV

the yearly Annual Wellness Visit

When

At least 12 months after the last AWV

How often

Once per 12-month period

What it includes (updates the baseline)

  • Updates the HRA, medical and family history, providers list, screening schedule, and risk factor list
  • Measures weight and blood pressure
  • Cognitive impairment is checked by the provider (Mini-Cog when applicable); personalized health advice and advance care planning are updated at the patient's discretion
MA noteThe work here is updating, not establishing. This is the visit most of your established Medicare patients return for each year. Still not a physical exam.

At rooming

How to tell which visit it is

  • Start with the scheduled visit type in Cerner, then confirm it fits the patient's Medicare timeline.
  • New to Medicare, still in the first year of Part B? Likely an IPPE.
  • Past 12 months of Part B, never had a wellness visit here? Likely a first AWV.
  • Comes in every year for the wellness visit? A subsequent AWV.
  • When it is unclear, ask the provider or front desk rather than guessing.

A few things to hold onto

  • One IPPE, ever, and only in the first 12 months of Part B. After that window it is an AWV.
  • First vs subsequent AWV matters: the first establishes the baseline, the yearly one updates it.
  • A wellness visit is not a physical exam. Depending on the issue, the provider may also address a problem during the visit.
  • Coding the visit and the Mini-Cog are the provider's responsibility, not the MA's. Your focus is recognizing the visit and rooming the patient well.

Knowledge check

Ten questions

Answer the questions, then choose Check my answers to see the correct answers and your score. Print the page and submit it to Jo.

  1. 1. The IPPE ("Welcome to Medicare") can be provided only during which window?

    Correct: B. The IPPE is only available in the first 12 months after Part B coverage starts.

  2. 2. How many times in a patient's lifetime can the IPPE be performed?

    Correct: A. The IPPE is a once-per-lifetime visit.

  3. 3. Which visit uniquely includes a visual acuity screen and a balance and gait check?

    Correct: C. Only the IPPE includes a limited exam with a visual acuity screen and a balance and gait check.

  4. 4. The first AWV establishes the prevention baseline. The subsequent AWV mainly:

    Correct: B. The first AWV establishes the baseline; subsequent AWVs update it.

  5. 5. How often can a subsequent AWV be completed?

    Correct: B. A subsequent AWV is completed once per 12-month period.

  6. 6. A Medicare wellness visit is a head-to-toe physical exam.

    Correct: B (False). A wellness visit is a prevention and screening visit, not a physical exam.

  7. 7. How many MAWV forms are there in Cerner?

    Correct: A. Cerner uses a single MAWV form for all of these visits; the form does not change by type.

  8. 8. Who is responsible for the visit's billing code?

    Correct: B. The provider, not the MA, is responsible for the billing code.

  9. 9. Who completes the Mini-Cog when it is applicable?

    Correct: B. The provider completes the Mini-Cog when applicable, not the MA.

  10. 10. A patient has had Part B for 3 years and has never had a wellness visit at your clinic. They are most likely due for:

    Correct: B. Past the first 12 months of Part B with no prior wellness visit, the patient is due their first AWV. The IPPE window has closed.
